Probability spaces, random variables, vectors

  • Recall the definitions of a probability space, random variable, and probability distribution.

  • Random vector of finite dimension: Given a probability space \((\Omega,\mathcal{F},P)\) a function \(X:\Omega \to \mathbb{R}^k\), \(k<\infty\) that is \(\langle \mathcal{F},\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{R}^k)\rangle\)-measurable is called a random vector of dimension \(k\). We may write \(X = (X_1,\dots,X_k)\).

  • Joint and marginal probability distributions: Let \(X = (X_1,\dots,X_k)\) be a random vector of dimension \(k\) on a probability space \((\Omega,\mathcal{F},P)\). Then:

    • The probability measure \[ P_X(A) = P(\{\omega : X(\omega) \in A\}), \quad A \in \mathcal{B}(\mathbb{R}^k) \] is called the joint probability distribution of \(X = (X_1,\dots,X_k)\).
    • For each \(j = 1,\dots,k\), the probability measure \[ P_{X_j}(A) = P(\{\omega : X_j(\omega) \in A\}), \quad A \in \mathcal{B}(\mathbb{R}^k)\] is called the marginal distribution of \(X_j\).

  • Joint and marginal cumulative distribution functions: Let \(X = (X_1,\dots,X_k)\) be a random vector of dimension \(k\) on a probability space \((\Omega,\mathcal{F},P)\). Then:

    • The function \[ \begin{align} F_X(x_1,\dots,x_k) &= P_X((-\infty,x_1] \times \dots \times (-\infty,x_k]) \\ &= P(\{\omega : X_1(\omega) \leq x_1, \dots, X_k(\omega) \leq x_k\}) \end{align} \] is called the joint cumulative distribution function of \(X\).
    • For each \(j= 1,\dots,k\) the function \[ F_{X_j}(x_j) = P_{X_j}((-\infty,x_j]) = P(\{\omega : X_j(\omega) \leq x_j\}) \] is called the marginal cumulative distribution function of \(X_j\).

  • Note that \(F_{X_1}(x_1) = F_X(x_1,\infty,\dots,\infty)\) and so on.

  • Expected value: Let \(X\) be a random variable on a probability space \((\Omega,\mathcal{F},P)\). Then the expected value of \(X\) is defined as \[ \mathbb{E}X \equiv \int X dP, \] provided at least one of the integrals \(\int X^+dP\) and \(\int X^{-}dP\) is finite. We often call \(\mathbb{E}X\) the mean of \(X\) and denote it as \(\mu_X = \mathbb{E}X\).

  • Change of variable formula: Let \(X\) be a random variable on a probability space \((\Omega,\mathcal{F},P)\) and let \(Y = h(X)\), for some Borel measurable function \(h: \mathbb{R}\to \mathbb{R}\). If \(\int |Y|dP < \infty\) then \[ \int Y dP = \int h(x) dP_X(x) = \int y dP_Y(y). \]

  • Moments of a random variable: Given a random variable \(X\), the quantity \(\mu_k = \mathbb{E}X^k\) is called the \(k\)th moment of \(X\) for \(k=1,2,\dots\)

  • Variance of a random variable: The variance of a random variable \(X\) is defined as \(\operatorname{Var}X = \mathbb{E}X^2 - (\mathbb{E}X)^2 = \mu_2 - \mu_1^2\).

  • Covariance between two random variables: Let \(X\) and \(Y\) be random variables on \((\Omega,\mathcal{F},P)\). Then the covariance between \(X\) and \(Y\) is defined as \(\operatorname{Cov}(X,Y) = \mathbb{E}XY - \mathbb{E}X \mathbb{E}Y\).

  • Moment generating function of a random variable: The moment generating function of a random variable \(X\) is defined as \[ M_X(t) = \mathbb{E}e^{tX}, \quad t \in \mathbb{R}. \] Note that the expectation may not be finite.

  • Relation between the moment generating function and moments: Let \(X\) be a random variable with moment generating function \(M_X(t)\) such that \(M_X(t) < \infty\) for all \(|t| < \varepsilon\) for some \(\varepsilon > 0\).

    1. Show that \(E|X|^k < \infty\) for all \(k=1,2,\dots\)
    2. Show that \(M_X(t) = \sum_{k=0}^\infty t^k \frac{\mu_k}{k!}\) for all \(|t| < \varepsilon\).
    3. Show that \(M_X(t)\) is infinitely differentiable on \((-\varepsilon,\varepsilon)\) with \[ M_X^{(r)}(t) = \sum_{k=0}^\infty \frac{t^k}{k!}\mu_{k + r} = \mathbb{E}X^k e^{tX} \] for \(|t| < \varepsilon\), whereby \(M^{(r)}_X(0) = \mu_r\).

    See Proposition 6.2.3 on page 195 of A&L.

  • Inequalities: Let \(X\) and \(Y\) be random variables on \((\Omega,\mathcal{F},P)\).

    • Markov’s: Show that \[ P_X(|X| > \epsilon) \leq \frac{\mathbb{E}|X|}{\epsilon} \] for each \(\epsilon > 0\).

    • Chebychev’s: Show that \[ P_X(|X - \mu_X| > k \sigma_X) \leq \frac{1}{k^2} \] for each \(k >0\), provided \(\sigma_X\) is finite.

    • Hölder’s: Show that \[ \mathbb{E}|XY| \leq (\mathbb{E}|X|^p)^{1/p} (\mathbb{E}|Y|^{q})^{1/q} \] for \(p,q \in (1,\infty)\) with \(1/p + 1/q = 1\), provided the expectations on the right hand side are finite.

    • Cauchy-Schwarz: Show that \[ \operatorname{Cov}(X,Y) \leq \sqrt{\operatorname{Var}X} \sqrt{\operatorname{Var}Y}, \] provided the variances on the right hand side are finite.

    • Minkowski’s: Show that \[ (\mathbb{E}|X+Y|^p)^{1/p} \leq (\mathbb{E}|X|^p)^{1/p} + (\mathbb{E}|Y|^p)^{1/p} \] provided the expectations on the right hand side are finite.

  • For a definition of joint moments and the joint moment generating function for the components of a random vector, see Definition 6.2.12 on page 198 of A&L.

Independence

  • Note that in a probability space \((\Omega, \mathcal{F}, P)\) we tend to call subsets of \(\mathcal{F}\) events and interpret \(P(A)\) for \(A \in \mathcal{F}\) as the probability of the event \(A\).

  • Independence among finitely many events: Let \((\Omega,\mathcal{F},P)\) be a probability space and let \(A_1,\dots,A_k \in \mathcal{F}\), \(1 \leq k < \infty\). Then

    • \(A_1,\dots,A_k\) are independent with respect to \(P\) if \[ P(\bigcap_{j=1}^l A_{i_j}) = \prod_{j=1}^l P(A_{i_j}) \] for each subset of indices \(\{i_1,\dots,i_l\} \subset \{1,\dots,n\}\), \(2 \leq l \leq k\).
    • \(A_1,\dots,A_k\) are pairwise independent with respect to \(P\) if \[ P(A_i \cap A_j) = P(A_i) P(A_j) \] for all \(1 \leq i < j \leq k\).

  • Independence of arbitrarily indexed events: For a nonempty set \(I\), a collection of events \(\{A_\alpha\}_{\alpha \in I}\) in a probability space \((\Omega,\mathcal{F},P)\) is independent with respect to \(P\) if for every finite set of indices \(\{\alpha_1,\dots,\alpha_k\} \subset I\) the events \(A_{\alpha_1},\dots,A_{\alpha_k}\) are independent with respect to \(P\).

  • Independence of finitely many collections of events: A set of collections \(\mathcal{A}_1,\dots,\mathcal{A}_k\) of events on a probability space \((\Omega,\mathcal{F},P)\) is independent with respect to \(P\) if every collection of events \(A_1,\dots,A_k\) with \(A_j \in \mathcal{A}_j\) for \(j = 1,\dots,k\) is independent with respect to \(P\).

  • Independence of arbitrarily indexed collections of events: For nonempty set \(I\), an arbitrarily indexed family \(\{\mathcal{A}_\alpha\}_{\alpha \in I}\) of collections of events on a probablity space \((\Omega,\mathcal{F},P)\) is independent with respect to \(P\) if every collection of events \(\{A_\alpha\}_{\alpha \in I}\) with \(A_\alpha \in \mathcal{A}_\alpha\) for \(\alpha \in I\) is independent with respect to \(P\).

  • Note that we need language for a set of sets and for a set of sets of sets. In these notes we have most of the time called a set of sets a collection of sets or a class of sets. For a set of sets of sets, we have used, in the above, a family of collections of sets. This language is arbitrary. Billingsley (on page 55, for example), introduces a “collection of classes of sets”.

  • Independence of finitely many random variables: A finite collection \(X_1,\dots,X_k\) of random variables on a probability space \((\Omega,\mathcal{F},P)\) is independent with respect to \(P\) if the collections of events \(\sigma \langle X_1\rangle,\dots,\sigma \langle X_k \rangle\) are independent with respect to \(P\).

  • Independence of an arbitrarily indexed family of random variables: For a nonempty set \(I\), a family \(\{X_\alpha\}_{\alpha \in I}\) of random variables on a probability space \((\Omega,\mathcal{F},P)\) is independent with respect to \(P\) if the family of \(\sigma\)-algebras \(\{\sigma \langle X_\alpha\rangle\}_{\alpha \in I}\) is independent with respect to \(P\).

  • Independence of \(\sigma\)-algebras generated by independent \(\pi\)-systems: Let \((\Omega,\mathcal{F},P)\) be a probability space and let \(\mathcal{A}_1,\dots,\mathcal{A}_k \subset \mathcal{F}\) be \(\pi\)-systems which are independent with respect to \(P\). Show that \(\sigma \langle \mathcal{A}_1\rangle,\dots, \sigma \langle \mathcal{A}_k \rangle\) are independent with respect to \(P\).

    See Proposition 7.1.1 on page 220 of A&L or Theorem 4.2 on page 55 of Billingsley. This can be extended to the case of an arbitrarily indexed family of \(\pi\)-systems: See Corollary 1 on page 56 of Billingsley.

  • Factorization rule: Show that for a nonempty set \(I\) the family \(\{X_\alpha\}_{\alpha \in I}\) of random variables is independent if and only if for every finite subset of indices \(\{\alpha_1,\dots,\alpha_k\} \subset I\) the joint CDF \(F_{\alpha_1,\dots,\alpha_k}\) of \((X_{\alpha_1},\dots,X_{\alpha_k})\) is equal to the product of the marginal CDFs, that is \[ F_{\alpha_1,\dots,\alpha_k}(x_1,\dots,x_k) = \prod_{j=1}^kF_{\alpha_j}(x_j) \] for all \(x_1,\dots,x_k \in \mathbb{R}\).

    Follow Corollary 7.1.2 of A&L. Prove by showing \(\{X^{-1}((-\infty,x]), x\in \mathbb{R}\}\) is a \(\pi\)-class and invoking the previous result.

  • Expectation of a product: Let \(X_1,\dots,X_k\), \(2 \leq k < \infty\), be random variables on a probability space \((\Omega,\mathcal{F},P)\).

    1. Show that \(X_1,\dots,X_k\) are independent if and only if \[ \mathbb{E}\prod_{j=1}^k h_j(X_j) = \prod_{j=1}^k \mathbb{E}h_j(X_j) \] for all bounded Borel measurable functions \(h_j:\mathbb{R}\to \mathbb{R}\), \(j=1,\dots,k\).

    2. Show that if \(X_1\) and \(X_2\) are independent and \(\mathbb{E}|X_1| <\infty\) and \(\mathbb{E}|X_2| < \infty\) then \(\mathbb{E}|X_1 X_2| < \infty\) and \[ \mathbb{E}X_1 X_2 = \mathbb{E}X_1 \mathbb{E}X_2. \]

    Set of random variables independent if expected value of product of functions is equal to the product of expectations—for all Borel measurable functions. Expectation of a product is the product of expectations. Follow Prop 7.1.3 on page 221 of A&L.
